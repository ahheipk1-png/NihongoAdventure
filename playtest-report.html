Playtest report · 22 August 2026

Nihongo Adventure: what a child actually experiences

A hands-on review of the live build at nihongoadventure.pages.dev — played through the real interface, with the game's own difficulty maths simulated against realistic child answering speeds.

The short version. The foundations are good: onboarding takes four taps, progress saves reliably, all twelve topics work, and the new illustrations land well.

The playtest found one genuinely serious bug, which I fixed: on a phone or tablet the hosted game was laying itself out at desktop width and shrinking the whole thing to about 38%. Every piece of responsive design was inert and every button was rendering at roughly a quarter of its intended size — on the devices your children are most likely to use.

A second serious problem is also now fixed: Memory Pairs was demoting kana a child already knew, because forgetting where a card was ran through the same code path as getting the answer wrong. It has become Pair Up — one side of every pair stays open, so the round tests Japanese instead of spatial recall.

What remains are two things in the reward layer. The Ninja tier is close to unwinnable at a realistic answering pace, and the result screen contradicts itself, telling a child they passed when they did not advance.

How this was tested

I played the live site directly: created a profile through the interface, worked through levels, answered correctly and deliberately incorrectly, triggered every result state, and inspected what the game did in response. Where a question was quantitative — "can a seven-year-old actually beat this?" — I ran the game's own constants through a simulation of thousands of rounds rather than guessing.

One honest limitation. The browser in this session could not composite frames, so I could not take screenshots or judge how anything looks, nor test real touch ergonomics on a tablet. Everything below is about behaviour, timing and structure — all of it exercised through the game's real event handlers, not simulated shortcuts. Visual polish and thumb-feel still need your eyes. It also means one thing I flagged as suspicious during play — Memory Pairs appearing to cap at 5 of 10 — turned out to be my test harness, not the game; it re-deals correctly and reaches 10/10. I mention it because it is the sort of false alarm worth not passing on to you.

What is working — leave these alone

Solid

The foundations are genuinely good

  • Onboarding. Four taps from a cold page to answering the first question, with no jargon. A parent could set up two children in under a minute.
  • Persistence. Verified on the real origin: name, avatar, tier, stars, per-topic levels and every Leitner box restored exactly after a reload — in 246 bytes. This was the one thing I could never confirm inside the Artifact sandbox.
  • The study sheet. Showing exactly what a level will ask, before it asks, is the single best pedagogical decision in the build. The grammar version — the sentence with its answer filled in and highlighted, plus the English — is excellent.
  • Pausing is fair. Power freezes during the Memory Pairs peek and while the mid-game Learn sheet is open, and the whole clock stops when the tab is hidden. A child who gets called away is never punished for it.
  • Sprout is correctly gentle. 100% win rate at every answering speed tested, no penalty for wrong answers, unlimited retries. For a pre-reader that is the right call.
  • The illustrations. Now used in five places, with a single shared image map so they cost no extra download, and a clean emoji fallback where no picture exists.

Findings, ranked by what a child would feel

CriticalFixed

1. On a phone or tablet, the whole game was being shrunk to 38%

The hosted build is a bare HTML fragment, so the browser constructs its own <head> — and that generated head carries no viewport rule. Without one, a mobile browser lays the page out at a notional ~980 px and then scales the result down to fit the screen.

Evidence
Measured on the live site at a 375 px device width: documentElement.clientWidth was 981, and every mobile breakpoint — max-width:430, 460, 520 — evaluated false.
Why it matters
All the responsive work was dead on the devices that matter most: the single-column answer grid, the three-across bag row, the reflowing pairs grid. A 68 px answer tile was reaching a child's thumb at about 26 px. This is the primary way your children will play, and it was the least-tested path.
My mistake
Earlier in the build I saw a mobile resize still report 980 px and wrote it off as a quirk of the preview pane. It was this bug, in plain sight. It took an independent reviewer looking at the served HTML to catch it.
Fix
Applied and deployed: a viewport meta tag (plus color-scheme and a description). Re-measured after deploying — layout viewport 375 px, all breakpoints active, answer tiles now real 167×68 targets, no horizontal scroll.
Note
The Artifact copy supplies its own document head, so it was likely unaffected; the hosted site definitely was.
CriticalFixed

2. Memory Pairs corrupted the learning data

When two flipped cards do not match, the game calls credit(p, kana, false) — the same "you got this wrong" path used everywhere else. But a mismatch in a memory game is a slip about where a card is, not a failure to know the character.

Evidence
Measured live: with every kana seeded at box 4 (mastered), one deliberate mismatch drove き from box 4 → box 2 and listed it under "Worth another look" on the results screen. The child knew き perfectly.
Why it matters
Every Memory Pairs round injects false failures into the spaced-repetition model. The engine then drills kana the child already knows, the garden under-reports real progress, and the "weak items" list fills with noise. It also partly explains the mastery gap below.
Also
Only the first card of the mismatched pair is demoted, which is arbitrary — the second is untouched.
Fix applied
Rather than patch the symptom, the game itself changed: Memory Pairs is now “Pair Up”. One side of every pair stays face up all round — the Japanese side, so the script is in front of the learner the whole time — and the meanings are hidden. Tap a hidden card, then tap the card it belongs with.
Why that is the better fix
It removes the spatial-memory tax entirely, so a wrong answer is now a genuine knowledge error and demoting the box is correct rather than noise. Three further improvements fell out of it: the demotion lands on the hidden card that was actually being identified (not an arbitrary one), tapping two open cards moves the selection instead of scoring a miss, and the ten-second peek is gone along with the memory load that needed it.
Measured after
Win rate at a 4-second pace rose from 70% → 100% (Explorer) and 23% → 61% (Ninja). 47 regression checks across every topic pass clean.
Critical

3. The result screen contradicts itself

Winning a round and advancing a level are two independent systems, but they are presented as one message.

Evidence
Both states reproduced live. Lost but promoted: a red "2 / 10" with "Power ran out" shown alongside "🎉 Level 2 unlocked". Won but stuck: "10 / 10" with the words "Level passed — power held out" while the level counter did not move.
Why it matters
The second case states something untrue. A child who fills the quota three rounds running, is told "Level passed" each time, and stays on Level 1 has been given no way to understand what the game wants from them.
Fix
Separate the two sentences. Round outcome: "Quota filled!" / "Power ran out." Level outcome, only when it actually happens: "Level 2 unlocked." When a level does not advance, say why in child language — "A few of these still need practice."
Effort
Small — copy plus one conditional.
Major

4. Ninja is close to unwinnable

Ninja stacks a bigger quota (14), a faster drain (5.2/s), a smaller refill (15) and a harsher penalty (−12). Those compound.

Why it matters
Four seconds to read an unfamiliar kanji, scan six options and tap is a reasonable pace for a ten-year-old — and at that pace Ninja Match is won 19% of the time and Ninja Fishing 2%. The tier meant for the oldest child is the one they will abandon.
Fix
Ninja drain 5.2 → 4.2, refill 15 → 18, penalty 12 → 8. Keep the quota at 14 so it stays the longest tier — length is a fairer way to be "harder" than a stopwatch.

5. Fishing Pond is the odd one out in every tier

Fishing is the only game at a 1.0× drain multiplier; the others sit at 0.55–0.75×. On top of that it is the only game where you must also track and hit a moving target, a cost the simulation does not even capture.

Evidence
A catch must land every 4.5 s (Explorer) or 2.9 s (Ninja) just to break even, from a starting runway of only 25 seconds. Explorer Fishing drops to 9% at a 6-second pace while Match is still at 79%.
Why it matters
Because the level assigns the game, a child cannot avoid it. Hitting a Fishing level after clearing four others feels like the game turning on them.
Fix
Lower the Fishing multiplier from 1.0 to 0.8, and give it a larger refill than the calm games — it should reward speed, not require it.
Effort
Trivial — one number.
Major

6. Finishing a topic does not mean knowing it — and nothing says so

A level advances when 80% of its items reach box 2. The garden calls an item mastered at box 4. Those two bars are far apart, and only one of them gates progress.

28%hiragana mastered at "completion" (Sprout)
~9 minto complete Animals, at 35% mastered
Why it matters
A child reaches the last level of hiragana in about 36 minutes having truly mastered a little over half of it — then the game says "Level 23 of 23" and offers "Play Level 23 →" forever. There is no completion message, no "come back and finish these nine", nothing that distinguishes finished the ladder from learned the kana.
Fix
Two small things. Raise the advance bar for review levels to box 3. And when courseDone() is true, replace the play card with a real completion state that names the items still below mastery and offers a "polish these" round.
Effort
Small to medium.
Minor

7. Match punishes a retry that Fruit Basket rewards

In Match, answering wrongly and then correctly gives no score and no power — the code runs bump() but never powerGain(). In Fruit Basket the same sequence awards both.

Evidence
Measured live, side by side: Fruit wrong-then-right → score 1. Match wrong-then-right → score 0.
Who it hurts
Sprout most, because Sprout is the tier with retries switched on. A four-year-old who gets everything right on the second attempt watches the counter stay at zero. Simulated Sprout Match still wins 86% of rounds even at 40% first-try accuracy, so this is unfairness rather than a blocker — but it is felt precisely by the child least able to shrug it off.
Fix
Award partial credit on a retry-success — half power, and count the quota — or make Fruit behave like Match. Either is fine; they should not disagree.
Effort
Trivial.
Minor

8. Two hours of kana before the fun topics open

Topics unlock strictly in order, so Animals — the topic with the best illustrations and the most obvious appeal to a young child — sits behind Hiragana, Katakana and Kana Mix.

Evidence
At the measured ~2 rounds per level: Hiragana opens at 0 min, Katakana ~38, Kana Mix ~77, Numbers ~125, Animals ~138 min, Grammar ~303 min.
Mitigation that already exists
Settings has "Open every topic now", which fixes it completely — but it is buried in a per-child settings screen and a parent has no reason to know it is there.
Fix
Unlock the small vocabulary topics after hiragana rather than after Kana Mix, or surface the override on the Topics screen itself with a plain-language label.
Effort
Trivial.
Minor

9. Accessibility gaps worth an hour

  • No lang attribute. Assistive tech does not know the page is part-Japanese, so kana get read with an English voice. (A color-scheme hint went in with the viewport fix; lang still wants adding.)
  • No live region. Score and power changes are never announced — a screen-reader user has no idea the clock is draining.
  • Right and wrong are signalled by colour (green/red) plus a shake, with no tick or cross. Add a glyph.
  • The utility buttons are 38 px tall — under the 44 px touch minimum. Game targets are comfortably fine; it is only Learn and End round.
  • The timed pressure cannot be turned off at Explorer or Ninja, which is a barrier for a child with slower motor or processing speed. A "no timer" toggle would cost nothing and would let one child play a tier that currently excludes them.

What is left to change

  1. Stop Memory Pairs demoting kana on a mismatch. Done — solved at the root by turning it into Pair Up.
  2. Split the round message from the level message. Small, and it removes the only place where the game says something untrue.
  3. Soften Ninja and Fishing. Four numbers, and the oldest child stops losing rounds they understood perfectly well.
